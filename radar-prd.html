01

Problem

There is no structured competitive intelligence function for AI assistants today. The market moves daily. The reporting does not.

Intelligence exists. It just dies in Teams threads.

What exists today

  • Ad hoc Teams messages when someone spots a competitor move.
  • Pre sales anecdotes that stay inside the deal they came from.
  • A newsletter that reports news rather than answering questions.
  • No owner, no cadence, no record.

What that costs

  • The same competitor move gets researched three times by three teams.
  • An objection heard in Madrid never reaches the marketer writing the battlecard.
  • Roadmap calls get made on the loudest anecdote in the room.
  • Nobody can answer what changed this month without a person going to look.

The shape of the market being tracked

AI assistants are moving from planning to booking to servicing. Planning is already commoditized across ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, Copilot and Mindtrip. Booking is the contested frontier, and it runs through rails, protocols and payments, which is precisely where we live. Servicing is wide open. A CI function that only tracks assistant launches will miss the part that decides the outcome.

02

Goals and non-goals

What this system is responsible for, and what it deliberately refuses to do.

Goals

  • One system of record. Three doors in, five ways out.
  • Coverage by category, not by volume. Five named categories decide what counts as signal.
  • Attention allocated by a published rubric. Scored, re-scored quarterly, overridden in real time by triggers.
  • A view per role, not a shared inbox. Each consumer lands on what their job needs.
  • Fact separated from judgment on every single record.
  • Habit before tool. Phase 1 runs with zero custom software.

Non-goals

  • Not a news aggregator. Volume is not the product.
  • Not a replacement for the analyst. The machine tags, the analyst confirms and signs.
  • Not an automated Assistant Lab. Hands on testing stays human on purpose.
  • Not a new BI stack. It rides whatever the team already standardizes on.
  • Not real time alerting on everything. Only tier 1 triggers interrupt anyone.
A dashboard nobody feeds is a graveyard. Habit first, tool second. The tool exists to make the habit cheap.

Architecture

Four layers. Ingestion, knowledge, intelligence, consumption. Click any layer for what it contains.

Two axis tagging

Every record carries a category (what part of the market it belongs to) and a door (how we came to know it). Filtering on both is what turns a feed into an answer. It also makes the honest gap visible: the Lab can only test platforms and part of payments, so rails, protocols and supplier moves are covered by the published and field doors only.

Why five categories and not a single feed
A single feed answers "what happened". Five categories answer "did the thing we are exposed to move". Booking without owning payment is a hollow win, so payments is tracked as a first class category rather than as a footnote under platforms. The reference move that proves the point: Sabre with Mindtrip and PayPal, announced February 2026, live May 2026. Three categories at once.
05

Data model

Five entities. Select one to see its fields.

The one rule that governs the schema. Facts and judgment never share a field. A Signal holds what happened and where it came from. An Assessment holds what we think it means, who thinks so, and when they signed it. Anyone reading a record can tell in one second which they are looking at.
06

Focus rubric

Not everyone earns the same attention. Here is how that gets decided, and re-decided.

Five steps. Published, so nobody has to guess why a competitor moved tier.

Step 1 · Score every player on three fixed questions

QuestionRange
Can they move a roadmap decision in the next 90 days?0 to 2
Do they touch a core revenue line? Distribution, IT solutions, hospitality, payments.0 to 2
Velocity. How fast are they actually shipping?0 to 2

Step 2 · Score sets the tier

TotalTierCadence
5 to 6Always-OnContinuous watch, in every digest
3 to 4WeeklyReviewed in the weekly digest
0 to 2MonthlySwept monthly, promoted on trigger

Scores are visible on every player row in the prototype as n/6, with the per question breakdown in the player profile.

Step 3 · Triggers override the schedule in real time

Funding or M&A
A round or an acquisition changes what a player can afford to do next.
Partnership or integration go-live
Distribution changes hands the day an integration ships, not the day it is announced.
Capability changing launch
Not a feature. A launch that moves a player across plan, book or service.
Direct field signal field
A competitor showing up in a live deal outranks anything published. Field flagged triggers sort to the top of the promotion queue.
built  The collector detects funding, partnership and launch triggers automatically and files them into the promotion queue. The last run flagged 7 launch triggers across Mindtrip, Sabre, Navan, TripGain and Travelport.

Step 4 · Depth is rationed inside the tier

Always-On earns attention. It does not automatically earn a Lab slot. Deep coverage, meaning full hands on Assistant Lab treatment, is reserved for roadmap movers. Everyone else gets light touch. Sabre is Always-On and light touch. Mindtrip is Always-On with Lab depth. That distinction is the whole point of the step.

Step 5 · Quarterly re-score

Every player is re-scored on the same three questions each quarter. Triggers catch the spikes. The re-score catches the slow fade and the slow rise, which is what actually kills a watchlist over time.

The agent

The published door is automated and running today. Click any stage for what it actually does.

Pipeline

Core logic in one screen

# 1. one query per watchlist subject, category attached up front
QUERIES = [("Mindtrip", '"Mindtrip" AI travel', "plat"), ...]   # 12 total

# 2. hard limits so the feed stays a signal, not a firehose
RECENT_DAYS = 45      # anything older is history, not intelligence
MAX_PER_QUERY = 3     # no single player can flood the run
MAX_TOTAL = 14        # a human has to be able to read the whole thing

# 3. three filters, in order: recency, relevance, duplicate
if item.dt < cutoff: skip
if not any(k in text for k in RELEVANCE): skip
if norm_title(item.title) in seen: skip

# 4. tag category and promotion trigger
item.cat     = keyword_cat(item)     # pay, proto, supplier override the default
item.trigger = detect_trigger(item)  # funding | partnership | launch | None

# 5. optional second pass: Claude re-reads the whole batch
items, mode = claude_triage(items)   # falls back to keywords on any failure

Triage pass · what Claude adds

  • Drops the noise keyword matching keeps. Stock coverage, listicles, generic travel news.
  • Fixes the category when a keyword lands an item in the wrong bucket.
  • Rewrites the body as one crisp why it matters line for an analyst, instead of a repeated headline.
  • Tags triggers strictly. Real events only, not commentary about events.
Modelclaude-opus-5
OverrideRADAR_MODEL
Outputjson_schema
Refusal checkstop_reason == "refusal"
On any failureSilent fallback to keyword tagging

Known limits, stated on purpose

  • Keyword mode occasionally miscategorizes. A news roundup landed under Protocols in a real run.
  • Keyword mode does not merge duplicate stories. Two Navan MCP items survived the same run.
  • Trigger words over fire on launch adjacent verbs. The triage prompt exists to correct exactly this.
This is not a defect to hide. It is the argument for the design: the machine tags, the analyst confirms. Automation buys coverage. It does not buy the signature.

From signal to takeaway

A headline is not intelligence. This is the path a raw item walks before it can change a decision.

Fact

What happened, with a source and a date. Never edited to fit a narrative. This is what the machine is allowed to produce on its own.

Assessment

What we think it means and how sure we are. Authored by a person, drafted by AI when useful, always attributable.

Action

What somebody should now do differently. If a Move has no action column, it was news, not intelligence.

09

Permissions and governance

Two hard walls, and nothing else locked. Click any cell for the rule behind it.

Full access Conditional Metadata only, item visible but locked

Wall 1 · Field signal bodies

Visible to CI, Leadership, and the contributing team only. A pre-sales lead in EMEA sees EMEA bodies and sees that APAC signals exist without reading them. This protects the source and the deal. Remove the wall and the field door goes quiet within a quarter.

Wall 2 · Unsigned drafts

Visible to CI and Leadership only. Nothing unsigned circulates as fact. The moment the analyst signs, the record appears for every role at once. In the prototype you can watch that happen live.

Everyone sees the titles and metadata of locked items. Nobody has to guess what exists. A wall you cannot see feels like a system that is hiding things, which costs more trust than it protects.
Why Leadership gets full access rather than a summary
The default view for Leadership is a single screen, because that is what the job needs on a Tuesday morning. But every other page stays open to them. It is a choice, not a wall. An executive who cannot check the underlying record eventually stops trusting the summary, and then asks a person to go and look, which is the exact behaviour this system exists to replace.
